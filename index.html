Injection and display

The overlays come from this CT's own segmentations, so they line up with the image:
  • Coronary overlay: the tree from ImageCAS-X, including the tree that isn't injected.
  • Chamber overlay: chambers and great vessels from TotalSegmentator. Each is drawn as an outline where the beam grazes its wall edge-on, which is where it would form a border on a real image. Click a chip above to show or hide one.
  • Segment numbers: the SCCT 18-segment model. Proximal, mid and distal RCA are thirds of the RCA up to the crux, because the acute marginal isn't labelled in this CT.
Brighter means nearer the detector. Hover over or tap a structure, here or in the room view, to name it. Over a chamber, the label also lists everything else in the beam at that point, detector side first.
How the views are named, and how to read the image
  • Views are named for where the detector sits. The X-ray tube is under the table, the detector is above the patient. LAO means the detector has swung over toward the patient's left; RAO, toward the right. Cranial tilts the detector toward the head, caudal toward the feet.
  • The image is drawn as seen from the detector. In AP the head is at the top and the patient's left is on the screen's right, like looking down at the patient from the ceiling.
  • RAO separates left from right along the AV groove. The AV groove (RCA and LCx) is seen edge-on, so the RCA looks like an "L" and the LAD runs along the front of the heart.
  • LAO looks down the septum. The AV groove opens into a ring, so the RCA looks like a "C", and the LAD and LCx split to either side of the septum.
  • Cranial views open the LAD and diagonals; caudal views open the LM, LCx and OMs. LAO caudal, the "spider", looks up the long axis from the apex onto the LM bifurcation.
  • The fluoro image is computed from one real coronary CT (a right-dominant heart), by tracing X-rays through it at the current angle. Real hearts vary in orientation, so this heart's best angles can differ by a few degrees from the textbook ones.
  • With the CT image, the anatomy overlay comes from that same CT: its coronary tree, chambers and great vessels. The aorta and pulmonary artery are cut off where the scan ends. If the CT can't load, a simplified, right-dominant model heart is used instead.
