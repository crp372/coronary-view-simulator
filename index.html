How to use

You're at the controls of a virtual cath lab. The big square is the fluoro monitor; the room view on the right shows where the C-arm and table are.

1. Choose a view

  • Pick a standard view from the list on the left (left coronary views at the top, right coronary below). The C-arm swings there and the injection side switches to match. Below the list, the current view is named with what it's best for.
  • Or set your own angle with the C-arm sliders: Rotation from RAO to LAO, Angulation from caudal to cranial. Field of view zooms the image (a smaller field is a bigger image).

2. Inject and run a cine

  • Left or Right under Injection chooses which coronary is filled with contrast, and which guide catheter is shown.
  • Run cine plays one injection: contrast streams down the arteries over a few beats, then washes out. The heart beats during the run (switch this off with Heart motion).

3. Move the table

  • Drag the image to pan the table, as you would to follow a vessel off the edge of the screen. The Table group has sliders for each direction and Centre table to reset.

4. Find out what you're looking at

  • Hover over a vessel (or tap it on a touch screen) to name it, in the image or the room view.
  • Under Display, Coronary tree overlays the whole tree, including the side that isn't injected; Chambers outlines the chambers and great vessels; Segment numbers shows the SCCT segments. Click a chamber's chip in the legend to show or hide it.
  • Vessel labels, Guide catheter, Fine branches and Spine and ribs switch those parts of the picture on and off.

5. Look at the room

  • Drag the room view to orbit around it and scroll to zoom. Close on heart moves the camera in on the heart inside the patient; Whole room goes back.

6. Try another heart

  • The Heart menu at the top switches between real CT hearts: right-dominant, left-dominant, co-dominant, and one with a narrowed mid LAD. The page reloads with the new heart.

Tips

  • Table and Display start collapsed: click a group's title to open or close it. Groups stay as you leave them, and closing the ones you aren't using gives the room view more space.
  • Full screen, or press F, hides the browser around the simulator.
  • To learn how views are named and how to read the image, see .

About this image

Swing the C-arm and move the table, then turn on the coronary and chamber overlays to see which structures the projection is showing you.

Where the image comes from

The overlays

With the CT image, the overlays come from this CT's own segmentations, so they line up with the image:
  • Coronary tree: from ImageCAS-X, including the tree that isn't injected.
  • Chambers: chambers and great vessels from TotalSegmentator. Each is drawn as an outline where the beam grazes its wall edge-on, which is where it would form a border on a real image. Click a chip in the legend to show or hide one.
  • Segment numbers: the SCCT 18-segment model. Proximal, mid and distal RCA are thirds of the RCA up to the crux, because the acute marginal isn't labelled in this CT.
Brighter means nearer the detector. Hover over or tap a structure, in the image or the room view, to name it. Over a chamber, the label also lists everything else in the beam at that point, detector side first.

How the views are named, and how to read the image

  • Views are named for where the detector sits. The X-ray tube is under the table, the detector is above the patient. LAO means the detector has swung over toward the patient's left; RAO, toward the right. Cranial tilts the detector toward the head, caudal toward the feet.
  • The image is drawn as seen from the detector. In AP the head is at the top and the patient's left is on the screen's right, like looking down at the patient from the ceiling.
  • RAO separates left from right along the AV groove. The AV groove (RCA and LCx) is seen edge-on, so the RCA looks like an "L" and the LAD runs along the front of the heart.
  • LAO looks down the septum. The AV groove opens into a ring, so the RCA looks like a "C", and the LAD and LCx split to either side of the septum.
  • Cranial views open the LAD and diagonals; caudal views open the LM, LCx and OMs. LAO caudal, the "spider", looks up the long axis from the apex onto the LM bifurcation.
  • The fluoro image is computed from one real coronary CT (a right-dominant heart), by tracing X-rays through it at the current angle. Real hearts vary in orientation, so this heart's best angles can differ by a few degrees from the textbook ones.
  • With the CT image, the anatomy overlay comes from that same CT: its coronary tree, chambers and great vessels. The aorta and pulmonary artery are cut off where the scan ends. If the CT can't load, a simplified, right-dominant model heart is used instead.
