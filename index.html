    How to use

    You're at the controls of a virtual cath lab. The big square is the fluoro monitor; the room view on the right shows where the C-arm and table are.

    1. Choose a view

    • Pick a standard view from the list on the left (left coronary views at the top, right coronary below). The C-arm swings there and the injection side switches to match. Below the list, the current view is named with what it's best for.
    • Or set your own angle with the C-arm sliders: Rotation from RAO to LAO, Angulation from caudal to cranial. Field of view zooms the image (a smaller field is a bigger image).

    2. Inject and run a cine

    • Left or Right under Injection chooses which coronary is filled with contrast, and which guide catheter is shown.
    • Run cine plays one injection: contrast streams down the arteries over a few beats, then washes out. The heart beats during the run (switch this off with Heart motion).

    3. Move the table

    • Drag the image to pan the table, as you would to follow a vessel off the edge of the screen. The Table group has sliders for each direction and Centre table to reset.

    4. Find out what you're looking at

    • Hover over a vessel (or tap it on a touch screen) to name it, in the image or the room view.
    • Under Display, Coronary tree overlays the whole tree, including the side that isn't injected; Chambers shades the chambers and great vessels; Segment numbers shows the SCCT segments. Click a chamber's chip in the legend to show or hide it, hover over a chip to pick that chamber out, or show just the left or right heart.
    • Vessel labels, Guide catheter, Fine branches and Spine and ribs switch those parts of the picture on and off.

    5. Look at the room

    • Drag the room view to orbit around it and scroll to zoom. Close on heart moves the camera in on the heart inside the patient; Whole room goes back.

    6. Try another heart

    • The Heart menu at the top switches between real CT hearts: right-dominant, left-dominant, co-dominant, and one with a narrowed mid LAD. The page reloads with the new heart.

    Tips

    • Table and Display start collapsed: click a group's title to open or close it. Groups stay as you leave them, and closing the ones you aren't using gives the room view more space.
    • Full screen, or press F, hides the browser around the simulator.
    • To learn where the image comes from and how to read the overlays, see .

    About this image

    Swing the C-arm and move the table, then turn on the coronary and chamber overlays to see which structures the projection is showing you.

    The fluoro image

    The fluoro image is computed from one real coronary CT (a right-dominant heart), by tracing X-rays through it at the current angle. Real hearts vary in orientation, so this heart's best angles can differ by a few degrees from the textbook ones.

    The overlays

    With the CT image, the overlays come from this CT's own segmentations, so they line up with the image. There are three of them:

    • Coronary tree. The arteries are labelled from ImageCAS-X. The tree that isn't injected is shown too, so you can see where it runs.
    • Chambers and great vessels. These come from TotalSegmentator. Each is drawn as its shadow in the beam, with one border around it: the left heart (LV, LA, aorta) in reds, the right heart (RV, RA, pulmonary artery) in blues. The LV includes its muscle wall, so its border is the outer surface that the LCx and its marginal branches run on. The other chambers and the great vessels are shown as their blood-filled cavities only, without their thin walls, so the RCA in its groove runs a few millimetres outside their borders. Where chambers overlap, the one nearest the detector is shaded, and a border that runs behind another chamber is dashed. Hover over a chamber, or its chip in the legend, to pick it out; click a chip to show or hide it. The aorta and pulmonary artery are cut off where the scan ends.
    • Segment numbers. These follow the SCCT 18-segment model. The proximal, mid and distal RCA are thirds of the RCA up to the crux, because the acute marginal isn't labelled in this CT.

    Reading them

    Brighter parts of an overlay are nearer the detector. Hover over or tap a structure, in the image or in the room view, to name it. Over a chamber, the label also lists everything else in the beam at that point, starting from the detector side.
