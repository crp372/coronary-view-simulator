How this image is made

Each pixel's ray runs from the X-ray tube (70 cm from the isocentre) to its point on the detector (110 cm), summing attenuation through the CT: μ = 0.2/cm × (1 + HU/1000). The CT angiogram's blood-pool contrast was removed during export. Iodine is added back only in the injected tree's lumen, so the image shows I = e−∫μ on a log scale with automatic brightness, quantum noise, edge enhancement and a square flat-panel field. The iodine slider sets TUNE.iod for this page only (the simulator uses 21 000).

Known limits

  • The CT covers only 23 × 23 × 14 cm around the heart. Beyond it, the lungs, ribs, spine and diaphragm come from a synthetic chest fitted to this scan (illustrative).
  • The heart motion during a cine run is modelled, not this heart's measured motion. No breathing.
  • One real, right-dominant heart. Its best angles can differ by a few degrees from the textbook ones.
  • The thinnest distal vessels are about one voxel of the coronary volume (0.45 mm) wide, so they look slightly thicker than they are.
