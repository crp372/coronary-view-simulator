How this image is made

Each pixel's ray runs from the X-ray tube (70 cm from the isocentre) to its point on the detector (110 cm), summing attenuation through the CT: μ = 0.2/cm × (1 + HU/1000). The CT angiogram's blood-pool contrast was removed during export. Iodine is added back only in the injected tree's lumen, so the image shows I = e−∫μ on a log scale with automatic brightness, quantum noise, edge enhancement and a vignette.

—

Known limits

  • The CT covers only 23 × 23 × 14 cm around the heart. Beyond it the image fades into flat soft tissue, so there's no full rib cage, diaphragm or lung edge.
  • No guide catheter, heart motion or breathing yet.
  • One real, right-dominant heart. Its best angles can differ by a few degrees from the textbook ones.
  • The thinnest distal vessels are about one voxel (0.9 mm) wide, so they look slightly thicker than they are.
